PPF in India: Everything You Need to Know About Public Provident Fund

The Public Provident Fund has been India's most popular long-term savings instrument for decades — and for good reason. It combines government safety, tax-free returns, and EEE tax status. Here is your complete guide to PPF.

What is PPF?

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a long-term government savings scheme that offers:

  • Government-backed safety — zero risk of default
  • Currently 7.1% annual interest (compounded annually, reviewed quarterly)
  • EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) tax status — investment, interest, and maturity are all tax-free
  • 15-year lock-in period (with extension options)
  • Maximum deposit: ₹1.5 lakhs per year

The EEE Tax Status — Why It Matters

PPF is one of the very few EEE investments in India. This means:

  • Exempt: Investment qualifies for Section 80C deduction (up to ₹1.5 lakhs)
  • Exempt: Annual interest earned is completely tax-free
  • Exempt: Maturity amount is 100% tax-free

Compare this to FD where interest is fully taxable, or NPS where 40% must be annuitised and the income is taxable. PPF's triple exemption is a significant advantage for those in the 20–30% tax bracket.

PPF Returns: What You Actually Earn

₹1.5 lakhs/year for 15 years in PPF at 7.1%:
Total invested: ₹22.5 lakhs
Maturity value: ~₹40.7 lakhs
Tax-free gain: ~₹18.2 lakhs
Effective post-tax return: 7.1% (vs FD where 7% becomes ~4.9% post-tax at 30% bracket)

How to Open a PPF Account

PPF accounts can be opened at:

  • Any post office in India
  • Nationalised banks (SBI, PNB, Bank of Baroda, etc.)
  • Some private banks (ICICI, Axis, HDFC — online opening available)

Documents needed: PAN card, Aadhaar, passport photo, and initial deposit (minimum ₹500). The process takes 30–60 minutes in person, or can be done online with some banks.

PPF Deposits: Important Rules

  • Minimum deposit: ₹500 per year (account becomes inactive if not deposited)
  • Maximum deposit: ₹1,50,000 per year (financial year April–March)
  • Deposit before 5th of each month to earn interest for that month
  • Lump sum deposit in April (beginning of financial year) maximises interest earned

PPF Loan and Withdrawal Rules

Loan Against PPF

Available from Year 3 to Year 6. You can borrow up to 25% of balance at end of second preceding year, at 1% above the PPF rate. Must be repaid within 36 months.

Partial Withdrawal

From Year 7, you can withdraw up to 50% of balance at the end of 4th preceding year, once per year. Tax-free.

Premature Closure

Only allowed after 5 years, for specific reasons — serious illness, higher education. A 1% interest penalty applies.

Extending PPF Beyond 15 Years

After 15 years, you can:

  • Close and withdraw: Take the full amount tax-free
  • Extend without deposits: Continue earning 7.1% on existing balance
  • Extend with deposits: Continue depositing up to ₹1.5 lakhs/year in 5-year blocks. Each extension must be requested within 1 year of maturity.

Extending with deposits is usually the best option — you maintain all tax benefits and keep compounding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I open multiple PPF accounts?

No. An individual can only have one PPF account in their own name. You can additionally open one in the name of a minor dependent, but the combined deposit across both cannot exceed ₹1.5 lakhs.

Q: Can NRIs invest in PPF?

Existing PPF accounts of NRIs who opened them while resident can be continued till maturity. Fresh PPF accounts cannot be opened by NRIs.

Q: Is PPF interest rate fixed?

No, the rate is reviewed quarterly by the government. It has ranged from 7.1% to 12% historically. Changes are rare but do happen. Your existing deposits earn the new rate from the change date.
