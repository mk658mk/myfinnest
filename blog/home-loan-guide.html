Home Loan in India: Everything You Need to Know Before Borrowing

Buying a home is the biggest financial decision most Indians ever make. A home loan will likely be your largest debt and span 20+ years of your life. Getting it right from the start can save you lakhs. Here is a complete guide.

Should You Buy or Rent?

Before taking a home loan, honestly evaluate this question. Renting is often financially better in cities where property prices are high relative to rents. A simple check: if the ratio of property price to annual rent is above 20 (i.e., price-to-rent ratio > 20), renting and investing the difference is often superior. In Indian metros, this ratio often exceeds 30–40.

That said, homeownership has non-financial value — stability, security, and the psychological satisfaction of ownership. Include these in your decision.

How Much Loan Can You Afford?

Most banks allow EMI up to 50% of your monthly income. But just because you can borrow that much does not mean you should.

A healthier rule: Your total EMIs (home loan + car + personal) should not exceed 35–40% of your take-home pay. This leaves room for savings, emergencies, and lifestyle expenses.

For a ₹1 lakh/month take-home salary, your total EMIs should not exceed ₹35,000–40,000. At 8.5% for 20 years, this supports a loan of approximately ₹40–46 lakhs.

Key Home Loan Concepts

Floating vs Fixed Rate

  • Floating rate: Linked to RBI repo rate via MCLR or RLLR. Changes with monetary policy. Currently 8–9.5%. No prepayment penalty.
  • Fixed rate: Locked rate for 2–5 years, then reverts to floating. Slightly higher initially. Provides short-term EMI certainty.

For most long-term borrowers, floating rate loans have historically proven cheaper and are the preferred choice.

MCLR vs RLLR (Repo Rate Linked Lending Rate)

Most new home loans are linked to the repo rate (RLLR). When RBI cuts rates, your benefit is passed on more quickly compared to MCLR-linked loans. Always prefer RLLR-linked loans for new borrowings.

Documents Required for Home Loan

For Salaried Individuals

  • Latest 3 months salary slips
  • 6 months bank statements
  • Form 16 / ITR for last 2 years
  • Employment letter / appointment letter
  • PAN card, Aadhaar, address proof

For Self-Employed

  • ITR for last 3 years with CA-certified P&L and balance sheet
  • Business registration documents
  • 6–12 months bank statements (personal and business)

The Smart Home Loan Strategy

  1. Maximise your down payment: Aim for 30–40% down payment. Less loan = less interest
  2. Choose shorter tenure: 15 years over 20 saves lakhs — see our EMI Calculator
  3. Make annual prepayments: Invest your bonus or tax refund directly into loan prepayment
  4. Compare lenders: Even 0.25% rate difference saves 1–2 lakhs over a 20-year loan
  5. Get a balance transfer if rates drop: If you can get 0.5%+ lower rate, a balance transfer may save significantly (account for processing fees)

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What credit score do I need for a home loan?

Most banks require a minimum CIBIL score of 750 for the best home loan rates. Scores below 700 may result in rejection or higher interest rates. Check your score before applying.

Q: Can I get a home loan without income proof?

Banks require income documentation. Self-employed individuals with good ITR history can qualify. Bank statement loans are available at higher rates from some NBFCs.

Q: How does joint home loan work?

A joint home loan (typically with a spouse) allows higher loan eligibility and both borrowers can claim tax benefits independently. Both must be co-owners of the property.

Q: What is the processing fee for home loans?

Typically 0.25–1% of the loan amount. This is negotiable, especially for large loan amounts or if you have a strong credit profile and existing banking relationship.
