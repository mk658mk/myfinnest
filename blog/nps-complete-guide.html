NPS Complete Guide: Should You Invest in the National Pension System?

The National Pension System (NPS) is one of India's most tax-efficient retirement tools — yet it remains underused. This guide explains everything you need to know about NPS, its returns, tax benefits, and whether it is right for you.

What is NPS?

The National Pension System (NPS) is a voluntary, government-regulated retirement savings scheme that allows any Indian citizen between 18–70 years to contribute during their working life and build a retirement corpus. It is managed by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA).

Unlike EPF (which is only for salaried employees), NPS is open to everyone — employed, self-employed, or even homemakers.

How NPS Works

Your NPS contributions are invested across three asset classes:

  • Asset Class E (Equity): Invested in stocks and equity funds. Highest risk, highest return potential
  • Asset Class C (Corporate Bonds): Fixed income instruments — moderate risk, stable returns
  • Asset Class G (Government Securities): Lowest risk, lowest return — pure government bonds

You choose how to allocate across these (or use Auto Choice which adjusts automatically based on your age).

NPS offers one of the best tax-saving combinations available in India:

NPS at Retirement — Withdrawal Rules

When you reach 60, here is how you can access your NPS corpus:

  • 60% of corpus: Can be withdrawn as a tax-free lump sum
  • 40% of corpus: Must be used to purchase an annuity — which pays monthly pension for life
  • If total corpus is less than ₹5 lakhs, you can withdraw 100% as a lump sum

The compulsory 40% annuity is the main criticism of NPS — annuity rates in India are low (5–6%), and annuity income is taxable. However, the tax savings during accumulation more than compensate in most cases.

Should You Invest in NPS?

Invest in NPS if:

  • You want the additional ₹50,000 tax deduction (80CCD1B) beyond 80C
  • You are in the 20–30% tax bracket and want to maximise tax savings
  • You are a self-employed professional who lacks employer-provided EPF
  • You need a forced, disciplined retirement savings mechanism

Evaluate carefully if:

  • You are already maximising EPF, PPF, and 80C — the marginal benefit decreases
  • You dislike the 40% annuity lock-in at retirement
  • You want full liquidity (NPS is extremely illiquid before 60)

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I open an NPS account online?

Yes. You can open an NPS account online via eNPS (enps.nsdl.com) using Aadhaar and PAN. The process takes about 30 minutes.

Q: What is the minimum NPS contribution?

Tier I account requires a minimum ₹500 per contribution and ₹1,000 per year. There is no minimum for Tier II (but Tier II has no tax benefits).

Q: Can I withdraw from NPS before 60?

Partial withdrawal (up to 25% of your contributions) is allowed after 3 years for specific reasons like education, medical emergency, home purchase. Early exit before 60 requires 80% annuitisation of corpus.

Q: Is NPS better than ELSS for tax saving?

ELSS has a 3-year lock-in vs NPS's lock-in till 60. ELSS offers full flexibility at maturity. If you just need 80C savings, ELSS is more flexible. If you want the additional ₹50,000 deduction, NPS is the only option.
