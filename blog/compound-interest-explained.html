Compound Interest Explained: The Secret to Building Wealth

Albert Einstein reportedly called compound interest the eighth wonder of the world. Whether or not he said it, the sentiment is accurate. Compound interest is the single most powerful force in personal finance — and understanding it can change how you think about money forever.

What is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is interest earned on both your original principal AND the interest you have already accumulated. In simpler terms, your interest earns interest. This creates a snowball effect — the longer you invest, the faster your money grows.

Simple Interest vs Compound Interest

FeatureSimple InterestCompound Interest
Calculated onPrincipal onlyPrincipal + accumulated interest
GrowthLinearExponential
FormulaP × R × T / 100P × (1 + R/n)^(nT)
Best forShort-term loansLong-term investments
Example — ₹1,00,000 at 10% for 20 years:
Simple Interest: ₹1,00,000 + ₹2,00,000 interest = ₹3,00,000
Compound Interest (annually): ₹1,00,000 × (1.10)²⁰ = ₹6,72,750
The difference? Over ₹3.7 lakh — just from compounding!

The Rule of 72

The Rule of 72 is a quick mental trick to find out how long it takes to double your money. Simply divide 72 by your annual interest rate.

  • At 6% (PPF): money doubles in 72 ÷ 6 = 12 years
  • At 8% (Debt funds): money doubles in 72 ÷ 8 = 9 years
  • At 12% (Equity): money doubles in 72 ÷ 12 = 6 years

How Compounding Frequency Matters

Compounding can happen annually, semi-annually, quarterly, monthly, or even daily. The more frequent the compounding, the more you earn.

Why Starting Early Matters So Much

The biggest variable in compounding is time. Consider two investors — Priya and Raj:

  • Priya starts investing ₹5,000/month at age 25 and stops at 35 (10 years, ₹6 lakh invested)
  • Raj starts at 35 and invests ₹5,000/month until 60 (25 years, ₹15 lakh invested)

At 60, assuming 12% returns, Priya has ~₹3.2 crore while Raj has ~₹94 lakh. Priya invested less money but started earlier — and won by a massive margin.

Compound Interest in Everyday Indian Investments

Mutual Funds (SIP)

Your SIP returns are compounded as the fund reinvests dividends and your NAV grows over time. This is why long-term equity SIPs are so powerful.

PPF (Public Provident Fund)

PPF compounds annually at 7.1% (current rate). It is government-backed, tax-free, and an excellent long-term compounding tool.

NPS (National Pension System)

Contributions grow through market-linked compounding over decades — ideal for retirement.

Fixed Deposits

FDs offer quarterly compounding typically. Safe but lower returns than equity over the long run.

The Dark Side — Compound Interest on Debt

Compounding works against you just as powerfully when you are in debt. A credit card debt of ₹50,000 at 3% monthly interest will compound to over ₹1.8 lakh in just 3 years if unpaid. This is why high-interest consumer debt must be cleared as fast as possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Which Indian investment has the best compounding?

Equity mutual funds have historically delivered the highest long-term compounding (12–15% p.a.) but carry market risk. PPF and NPS offer safer, tax-efficient compounding.

Q: Is compound interest better than simple interest?

For investors, yes — always. For borrowers, compound interest means you owe more, so simple-interest loans are preferable from the borrower's side.

Q: What is the formula for compound interest in India?

A = P × (1 + R/n)^(nT) where P = Principal, R = annual rate, n = compounding frequency per year, T = time in years.
