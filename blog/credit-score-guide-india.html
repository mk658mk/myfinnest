CIBIL Credit Score Guide: Check, Understand & Improve Your Score

Your credit score is a three-digit number that silently determines whether you get a home loan, what interest rate you pay, and even whether some employers hire you. Yet most Indians have never checked theirs. This guide fixes that.

What is a CIBIL Score?

A CIBIL score (also called a credit score) is a three-digit number ranging from 300 to 900 that represents your creditworthiness. It is calculated by TransUnion CIBIL — India's oldest and most widely used credit bureau — based on your borrowing and repayment history.

Banks and NBFCs use this score to decide whether to approve your loan or credit card application and at what interest rate. India has three other credit bureaus as well — Equifax, Experian, and CRIF Highmark — and lenders may use any of them.

How Your Score is Calculated

CIBIL does not publish the exact weightings, but based on industry data, here is an approximate breakdown:

  • Payment History (~35%): Do you pay EMIs and credit card bills on time? Even a single 30-day late payment can drop your score by 50–100 points.
  • Credit Utilisation (~30%): How much of your available credit limit are you using? Staying below 30% of your total credit card limit is ideal.
  • Credit Age (~15%): The longer your credit history, the better. Closing your oldest credit card hurts this factor.
  • Credit Mix (~10%): Having a mix of secured loans (home, auto) and unsecured credit (cards, personal loans) looks better than only one type.
  • New Enquiries (~10%): Every time you apply for credit, the lender does a hard enquiry that temporarily dips your score. Multiple applications in a short period are a red flag.

How to Check Your CIBIL Score for Free

You are entitled to one free credit report per year from each bureau. Here is how:

  1. Visit cibil.com and click "Get Your Free CIBIL Score."
  2. Verify your identity with PAN, date of birth, and address.
  3. Answer a few authentication questions from your credit history.
  4. Your score and full credit report are displayed instantly.

Alternatively, apps like Paytm, BankBazaar, OneScore, and Bajaj Finserv offer free monthly score checks (these are soft enquiries and do not affect your score).

Check Once a Year Minimum: Review your full credit report — not just the score — for errors. Incorrect accounts, wrong addresses, or fraudulent credit card entries do appear and can silently damage your score. Raise a dispute directly on CIBIL's website to correct them.

10 Proven Ways to Improve Your Credit Score

✅

Pay on Time, Every Time

Set up auto-pay for at least the minimum due on credit cards. Never miss an EMI date.

📉

Keep Utilisation Below 30%

If your card limit is ₹1 lakh, keep your balance below ₹30,000 at all times.

🚫

Don't Apply for Multiple Loans at Once

Each hard enquiry dips your score. Space applications at least 6 months apart.

🕐

Keep Old Cards Active

Use your oldest credit card for a small monthly purchase to keep the account open and boost credit age.

🔍

Check for Errors Regularly

Dispute incorrect entries immediately. CIBIL must resolve disputes within 30 days.

🏦

Get a Secured Credit Card

If your score is very low or you have no credit history, a secured card (backed by an FD) builds credit fast.

💳

Request a Limit Increase

Increasing your credit limit without spending more reduces your utilisation ratio automatically.

📋

Settle Old Dues Completely

"Settled" accounts (where you paid less than owed) are worse on your report than fully cleared ones.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does checking my own score hurt it?

No. When you check your own score, it is a "soft enquiry" and has zero impact on your score. Only lender-initiated "hard enquiries" affect your score.

Q: I have no loans or credit cards — do I have a score?

You likely have no credit history at all, which is different from a bad score. Lenders call this "NH" (No History) or "NA." Start with a secured credit card or a small consumer loan to build your profile.

Q: How long does a default stay on my CIBIL report?

Seven years from the date of default. However, the impact on your score decreases over time, especially if you demonstrate good repayment behaviour afterward.

Q: My loan was rejected — will that show on my credit report?

The rejection itself does not appear, but the hard enquiry the lender made before rejecting you does. Too many hard enquiries in a short period are visible to future lenders.
