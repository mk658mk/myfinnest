Emergency Fund: How Much Do You Need and Where to Keep It?

An emergency fund is the single most important financial safety net you can have. Without it, any unexpected expense — a job loss, medical bill, or car repair — can derail years of financial progress. Here is how to build yours.

What is an Emergency Fund?

An emergency fund is a dedicated pool of money set aside exclusively for unexpected financial emergencies. It is not for planned expenses, vacations, or investments — it is purely a buffer against life's unpredictable events.

Why Every Indian Needs One

  • Job loss: Corporate layoffs, startup failures, business downturns can cut income overnight
  • Medical emergencies: Even with insurance, out-of-pocket medical costs can be significant
  • Family emergencies: Sudden travel, family illness, or support obligations
  • Car/home repairs: Unexpected but unavoidable large expenses
  • Income gaps: Freelancers, consultants, and business owners face irregular income

Without an emergency fund, you are forced to break your investments (losing compounding), take expensive personal loans (12–20% interest), or go into credit card debt (36%+ effective interest). The emergency fund prevents all of this.

How Much Do You Need?

Where to Keep Your Emergency Fund

The key criteria: safe, liquid, and accessible within 24 hours. Do not chase returns here — this is not an investment.

Best Options

  • High-yield savings account: Many banks offer 3.5–7% on savings accounts. Keep 1–2 months here for instant access.
  • Liquid mutual funds: Returns of ~6–7%, withdraw T+1 (next business day). Low risk, better than savings. Ideal for the bulk of your emergency fund.
  • Overnight funds: Even lower risk than liquid funds. Returns ~6%. Good for the conservative portion.
  • FD with sweep-in facility: Offers FD rates (~7%) with savings account liquidity. Available at most major banks.

Avoid These for Emergency Funds

  • Equity mutual funds (too volatile, may be down when you need money)
  • PPF (5-year lock-in, limited withdrawal)
  • Physical gold (illiquid, selling takes time)
  • Real estate (very illiquid)

How to Build Your Emergency Fund

  1. Calculate your monthly expenses (include EMIs, insurance premiums)
  2. Set a target (typically 6 months of expenses)
  3. Open a dedicated account — do not mix with your regular savings
  4. Set up an automatic transfer on salary day — even ₹3,000/month adds up
  5. Use any windfalls (bonus, tax refund) to top it up faster
  6. Once built, invest only the surplus above the target

After Building Your Emergency Fund

Once your emergency fund is fully funded, do not add more to it. The opportunity cost of keeping 12+ months of expenses in low-return accounts is significant. Redirect any surplus into your SIP or other investments.

Review your emergency fund size annually or when your expenses change significantly (new EMI, new family member, relocation).

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Should I build an emergency fund before starting SIP?

Yes — having at least 1–2 months of expenses saved first is recommended. You can build both simultaneously by splitting your savings — some to emergency fund, some to SIP. Do not delay investing entirely.

Q: My savings account gives only 3.5% — should I move my emergency fund to liquid funds?

Yes, liquid funds are ideal for the bulk of your emergency fund. Keep 1 month in a savings account for instant access, and park the rest in liquid or overnight funds.

Q: I used my emergency fund — what now?

Replenish it as your first financial priority. Treat it like a bill that must be paid. Pause non-essential investing if needed, but restore the emergency fund quickly.
