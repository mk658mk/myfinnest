SWP: How to Create a Monthly Income from Mutual Funds in Retirement

Most people focus only on building wealth before retirement — but an equally critical question is how to withdraw it smartly. A Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) from mutual funds is one of the most tax-efficient ways to generate regular retirement income in India.

What is SWP?

A Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) is the mirror image of a SIP. Instead of investing a fixed amount every month, you withdraw a fixed amount from your mutual fund corpus each month. The remaining corpus continues to grow and generate returns.

Done correctly, an SWP can provide a steady monthly income while your corpus remains intact — or even grows — over time.

How SWP Works

Example: You retire with ₹1 crore in a balanced mutual fund earning 10% p.a.
If you withdraw ₹50,000/month (₹6 lakhs/year = 6% withdrawal rate), your corpus would be exhausted in about 24 years.
If you withdraw ₹35,000/month (~4.2% withdrawal rate), your corpus could theoretically last indefinitely.

Why SWP is Tax-Efficient

This is the key advantage of SWP over bank interest or annuity income:

When you withdraw via SWP, each unit you redeem has two components — cost (what you paid) and gain (appreciation). You are taxed only on the gains portion, not the full withdrawal amount.

For equity funds held over 1 year, LTCG is taxed at just 10% (above ₹1 lakh annual). Compare this to FD interest which is taxed at your full income slab (up to 30%).

Monthly withdrawal ₹50,000 from equity fund (corpus bought 5 years ago):
Of ₹50,000 withdrawn, suppose ₹35,000 is original cost and ₹15,000 is gain.
Tax: only on ₹15,000 gain at 10% = ₹1,500 (effective tax rate on withdrawal = just 3%)
vs FD Interest: ₹50,000 income taxed at 30% = ₹15,000 in tax

The Ideal SWP Strategy

Bucket Strategy

Divide your retirement corpus into three buckets:

  • Bucket 1 (0–2 years): 2 years of expenses in liquid funds or savings. Zero market risk.
  • Bucket 2 (3–7 years): 5 years of expenses in balanced/debt-heavy funds. Moderate risk.
  • Bucket 3 (7+ years): Remaining corpus in equity funds. Generates long-term growth.

Run SWP from Bucket 1. As it depletes, refill from Bucket 2, which is refilled by Bucket 3 gains. This protects you from sequence-of-returns risk.

Choosing the Right Fund for SWP

Not all funds are ideal for SWP. Consider:

  • Balanced Advantage Funds (BAF): Dynamically adjusts equity-debt ratio. Offers growth with moderate stability. Popular for SWP.
  • Equity Savings Funds: Part equity, part debt, part arbitrage. Moderate volatility, relatively stable.
  • Debt Funds (short/medium duration): For the more conservative portion of your SWP setup.

Avoid pure equity large-cap funds for SWP if you need monthly income — market falls will force you to sell units at low prices, depleting your corpus faster.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can SWP be stopped or changed?

Yes. You can stop, change the amount, or change the frequency of an SWP at any time — making it far more flexible than annuities.

Q: What withdrawal rate is safe in India?

Given India's higher inflation (6%+), a 3–4% annual withdrawal rate is considered safer than the US-derived 4% rule. This means withdrawing ₹3–4 lakhs per year per ₹1 crore of corpus.

Q: Is there a minimum SWP amount?

Each fund house sets its own minimum — typically ₹500 to ₹1,000 per withdrawal. There is usually no maximum limit.
