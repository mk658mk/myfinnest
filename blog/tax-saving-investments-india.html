Best Tax-Saving Investments Under Section 80C in India (2024–25)

Section 80C is one of the most powerful tools available to Indian taxpayers. A ₹1.5 lakh annual deduction can save ₹15,000–₹46,800 in taxes depending on your bracket. But choosing the wrong 80C option can cost you dearly. Here is how to choose wisely.

What is Section 80C?

Section 80C of the Income Tax Act allows individual taxpayers and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) to claim a deduction of up to ₹1,50,000 from their taxable income in a financial year. This deduction is available only under the Old Tax Regime (not the new default regime).

All 80C Options: A Complete List

Investment Options

  • ELSS Mutual Funds: 3-year lock-in, equity returns (12–15% historical), market risk
  • PPF: 15-year lock-in, 7.1% guaranteed, completely tax-free (EEE)
  • NPS Tier I: Till age 60 lock-in, 10–12% historical, additional ₹50K deduction via 80CCD(1B)
  • Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY): 8.2%, EEE, only for girl children
  • Senior Citizens Savings Scheme (SCSS): 8.2%, 5-year tenure, only for 60+ years

Savings/Fixed Return Options

  • 5-Year Tax-Saving FD: 6.5–7%, 5-year lock-in, interest taxable
  • National Savings Certificate (NSC): 7.7%, 5-year lock-in, interest taxable
  • Employee Provident Fund (EPF): Auto-deducted for salaried, ~8.15%, tax-free on maturity

Life Insurance

  • Life insurance premiums (term, endowment, ULIP) qualify for 80C — but buy insurance for protection, not tax saving

Expenses and Repayments

  • Home loan principal repayment
  • Children's tuition fees (up to 2 children)
  • Stamp duty and registration for property purchase (one-time)

How to Optimise Your 80C Deductions

Step 1: Check What You Already Have

EPF deductions are automatic for salaried employees. Home loan principal, children's school fees also count. Add these up first — you may already be partially covered without additional investment.

Step 2: Fill the Gap Wisely

Recommended priority (Old Tax Regime):
1. Maximize EPF (automatic) + check school fees + home loan principal
2. Fill remaining limit with ELSS (for wealth creation) or PPF (for safety)
3. Add NPS ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B) for additional deduction beyond 80C

Old Tax Regime vs New Tax Regime

The new tax regime (default from FY2024–25) offers lower slab rates but removes most deductions including 80C. Whether to opt in depends on your total deductions:

  • If your total deductions (80C + HRA + home loan interest + 80D + others) exceed ₹3–3.5 lakhs, Old Regime usually saves more tax
  • If your deductions are limited, New Regime's lower rates may be better

Calculate both scenarios carefully — ideally with a CA — before choosing your regime for the financial year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I claim 80C deduction for my spouse's investments?

No, you can only claim deductions for investments made in your own name (or your minor child's name for some instruments). Your spouse must file their own 80C claims.

Q: Is the ₹1.5 lakh limit for all 80C combined or per instrument?

It is a combined limit across all 80C instruments. You cannot claim more than ₹1.5 lakhs in total deduction under 80C, regardless of how many instruments you invest in.

Q: I am in the new tax regime — what can I still deduct?

Under the new regime, you can claim: Standard deduction (₹75,000), NPS employer contribution (80CCD(2)), Agniveer corpus, and a few others. Most individual deductions including 80C are not available.
