Health Insurance in India: Complete Buyer's Guide

A single hospitalisation in a private Indian hospital can cost ₹3–15 lakhs. Without health insurance, one medical emergency can wipe out years of savings. Yet more than 70% of Indians remain uninsured or dangerously underinsured. This guide helps you fix that.

Why Health Insurance is Non-Negotiable

Medical inflation in India runs at approximately 14% per year — roughly double the general inflation rate. A surgery that cost ₹2 lakhs today may cost ₹7.4 lakhs in just 10 years. Company-provided group insurance, while helpful, typically provides only ₹3–5 lakhs of cover — woefully inadequate for serious illness.

An individual health insurance policy of ₹10–25 lakhs, bought young, is one of the most important financial moves you can make.

How Much Cover Do You Need?

The minimum recommended cover in a metro city (Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru, Chennai) today is ₹10 lakhs per person. Here is a simple sizing guide:

For family floaters, the sum insured should be adequate for the highest-risk member. If both spouses are above 45, individual policies are often better than a floater.

Key Terms You Must Understand Before Buying

Sum Insured

The maximum amount the insurer will pay per policy year. Choose wisely — under-insuring to save premium is a false economy.

Waiting Period

Most health policies have a 30-day initial waiting period (no claims except accidents), a 2–4 year waiting period for pre-existing diseases (PED), and a 1–2 year waiting period for specific illnesses like cataracts, hernia, and joint replacements. Buy early — every year you wait extends the period before you can claim for pre-existing conditions.

Cashless vs Reimbursement

Cashless hospitalisation means the insurer settles bills directly with the network hospital. Reimbursement requires you to pay upfront and claim later. Always prefer a cashless claim at a network hospital.

Co-payment

Some plans — especially senior citizen policies — require you to pay a percentage (10–30%) of every claim. This reduces premiums but increases your out-of-pocket cost during claims. Avoid co-payment clauses if possible.

Room Rent Limit

A cap on the daily hospital room rent (e.g. 1% of sum insured per day). If you choose a room above this limit, the insurer proportionally reduces the entire hospital bill. Always choose a plan with no room rent limit or a generous one.

No-Claim Bonus (NCB)

Most insurers reward claim-free years by increasing your sum insured (typically 5–50% per year) without additional premium. Policies with high NCB accumulation are valuable long-term.

Claim Settlement Ratio (CSR)

The percentage of claims an insurer settles. Look for insurers with a CSR above 95%. The IRDAI publishes annual CSR data for all insurers.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Buying only employer cover and assuming you are protected
  • Choosing a plan only based on lowest premium
  • Not disclosing pre-existing conditions (leads to claim rejection)
  • Ignoring room rent limits and co-payment clauses in the fine print
  • Buying a plan with a small, limited hospital network

The Smart Strategy: Base Plan + Super Top-Up

Instead of buying a very high sum insured plan at full cost, a cost-effective approach is:

Example for a 35-year-old in Bengaluru:
Base Plan: ₹5 lakh sum insured → Approx. ₹8,000–10,000/year premium
Super Top-Up: ₹20 lakh cover above ₹5 lakh deductible → Approx. ₹4,000–6,000/year
Effective cover: ₹25 lakhs at ₹12,000–16,000/year total

Buying a standalone ₹25 lakh plan would cost ₹22,000–30,000/year for the same person. You save ₹10,000+ per year with the combination approach.

✅ Health Insurance Buying Checklist

  • Choose minimum ₹10 lakh sum insured in a metro city
  • Opt for no room rent limit or at least 1% of sum insured cap
  • Check for no co-payment clause (especially for non-senior plans)
  • Verify network hospital list includes top hospitals in your city
  • Check the insurer's Claim Settlement Ratio (target: 95%+)
  • Disclose all pre-existing conditions honestly at the time of purchase
  • Consider a Super Top-Up plan for additional coverage at low cost
  • Look for plans with NCB (No-Claim Bonus) of 50%+ accumulation
  • Get parents covered separately — preferably before age 60
  • Review and renew your policy without break every year

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is it better to buy health insurance directly or through an agent?

Buying directly from the insurer's website is often cheaper (no agent commission built in) and transparent. Aggregator websites like PolicyBazaar or Ditto Insurance can help compare plans. However, an experienced advisor can be helpful for complex cases (senior parents, pre-existing conditions).

Q: Can I have two health insurance policies?

Yes. You can hold multiple policies. In the event of a claim, you can use one policy first and claim the remaining balance from the second. This is called "contribution" in insurance terms.

Q: At what age should I buy health insurance?

As early as possible — ideally in your 20s. Premiums are significantly lower when you are young and healthy, waiting periods for pre-existing conditions start ticking sooner, and you benefit from NCB accumulation for many more years.

Q: Will my employer's group insurance cover me if I resign?

No. Group insurance ends the day your employment ends. This is why having your own individual or family health policy independent of your employer is essential. Do not wait until you resign to buy — pre-existing condition waiting periods mean you need cover in place well in advance.
