Planning Your Child's Education Fund: A Complete Indian Parent's Guide

The cost of quality education in India is rising at 8–10% per year. A degree that costs ₹5 lakhs today will cost ₹15+ lakhs in 15 years. Smart parents start planning the day their child is born — here is how.

How Much Will Education Cost?

Education inflation in India runs at 8–10% annually — nearly double the general inflation rate. The costs vary dramatically by the type of education your child pursues:

These numbers are sobering — but not impossible with early planning.

The Power of Starting Early

Target: ₹30 lakhs in 15 years (for child born today)
If you start investing today at 12% returns: You need to invest just ₹7,400/month
If you start 5 years later (10 years remaining): You need ₹14,200/month
If you wait 10 years (only 5 years remaining): You need ₹38,400/month

Starting early cuts your required monthly investment by more than 5x. Time truly is the most valuable resource in financial planning.

Best Investment Options for Child's Education Fund

Equity Mutual Funds via SIP

The best option for goals that are 10+ years away. Choose a diversified large-cap or flexi-cap fund. Historically delivers 12–15% p.a. over long periods. As your child approaches college age (3–4 years away), gradually shift to debt funds to protect the corpus.

Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)

If you have a daughter, SSY is a must. Government-backed, currently offering 8.2% p.a. (tax-free), with a ₹1.5 lakh annual deposit limit. Matures when the girl turns 21. Only for girl children.

Public Provident Fund (PPF)

7.1% tax-free returns. 15-year lock-in aligns well with a child's higher education timeline. Open a PPF account in the child's name — deposits count toward your Section 80C limit.

Child Plans (ULIP/Endowment)

Insurance companies market "child plans" heavily. These combine insurance with investment, typically delivering poor returns (6–7%) after charges. Avoid these — buy a term plan separately and invest in mutual funds instead for far better outcomes.

The Glide Path Strategy

As your child's education goal approaches, you should gradually reduce risk:

  • 15+ years away: 100% equity mutual funds
  • 10–15 years away: 80% equity, 20% debt
  • 5–10 years away: 60% equity, 40% debt
  • 2–5 years away: 30% equity, 70% debt/liquid funds
  • 1–2 years away: Move entirely to liquid/short-term debt funds

Education Loans — When to Use Them

Even with planning, education loans can be a useful tool. Key points:

  • Interest on education loans is fully deductible under Section 80E (no upper limit) for 8 years
  • The student (not parent) is the primary borrower — it builds their credit history
  • Moratorium period during study means no EMI while studying
  • Only use for high-ROI education (engineering, medicine, MBA from a top institution)
  • Never take a loan for education with unclear career prospects

Practical Steps to Start Today

  1. Estimate the type of education your child might pursue and its future cost
  2. Start a dedicated SIP immediately — even ₹2,000/month makes a difference early on
  3. Open a SSY account if you have a daughter
  4. Review and increase your SIP annually as your income grows
  5. Keep this corpus completely separate — do not dip into it for other expenses
  6. Get adequate term insurance to protect the plan if something happens to you

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Should I invest in my child's name or my name?

Investing in your own name is usually better for tax efficiency, especially for equity funds. For SSY, investment must be in the child's name (operated by parents).

Q: What if my child does not pursue the education I planned for?

The corpus is yours — redirect it to the child's actual education, their startup, their wedding, or your own retirement. Staying invested is always better than not starting.

Q: Is SSY better than PPF for a girl child?

Yes. SSY currently offers 8.2% vs PPF's 7.1%, and both are tax-free. The main limitation is SSY's lower lock-in flexibility. Many parents use both — SSY + mutual fund SIP.
